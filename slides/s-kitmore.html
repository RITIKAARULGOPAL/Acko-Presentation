00 · Light, sound and technology

Light and sound, as one strategy.

Lighting · scenes, not fittings
  • Workhalls — tunable white, 4000 K by day to 3000 K at dusk; daylight-linked dimming on the west glass.
  • SOC — glare-controlled task light, a programmable halo in the ceiling arc, wall-wash behind the command wall.
  • Boardroom — Meeting · Presentation · Dining presets on one keypad.
  • Events — DMX-ready track along the event spine; a house-lights scene from reception.
Acoustics · privacy by design
  • Absorptive ceilings and walls at NRC 0.70–0.90; meeting-room ceilings tuned for reverberation.
  • Full-height partitions with STC ratings by adjacency; acoustic doors and double glazing.
  • HVAC to zone NC targets; sound masking in the open workhalls.
  • SOC: carpet, ceiling absorbers and a soft rear wall for speech privacy across 80 seats.
Biophilia · wellbeing
  • Planted edges at arrival, along the SOC glass and at every breakout.
  • Daylight kept for people: the core is on the east, desks run to the glass.
  • Ergonomic seating for 24 × 7 SOC shifts.
  • Low-VOC, formaldehyde-free materials throughout.
Invisible · in the architecture
  • One credential from turnstile to door to SOC airlock, mapped to the layers of trust.
  • Sensors for occupancy, CO₂ and temperature feed a live floor dashboard — the twin, for real.
  • Room booking with auto-release; every room VC-ready on one platform.
  • Resilient backbone — dual data paths and UPS-backed power for the SOC and server room.
Visible · only where it earns its place
  • The SOC command wall — the one big screen on the floor.
  • The arrival media wall — perforated metal pixels, not a TV.
  • Event screens that drop from the ceiling and disappear after.
  • The studio's ON AIR signal.

If a screen isn't telling a story, it doesn't go on a wall.
